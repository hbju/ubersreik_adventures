## Status

Old World society is rigidly stratified. Status governs how characters are perceived and treated across all social interactions.

### Tiers and Standing

**Status** is expressed as a **Tier** and a **Standing**:
- **Tier** — Brass (lowest), Silver, or Gold (highest).
- **Standing** — a number, generally 1–5, reflecting how respected a character is within their Tier.

A higher Tier always outranks a lower Tier, regardless of Standing. Within the same Tier, higher Standing wins.

---

### The Three Tiers

**The Gold Tier:** Rulers, their direct servants (advisors, protectors, law-keepers), and the independently wealthy. Rich, respected, and of higher Status than all Silver and Brass characters.

**The Silver Tier:** Well-to-do townsfolk, skilled professionals, tradespeople, craftspeople, and merchants. Respectable lives, but humble compared to Gold Tier. Higher Status than all Brass Tier characters.

**The Brass Tier:** Peasants, unskilled labourers, criminals, and those with no reliable income. The bottom rung of the social ladder.

---

### Determining Status

Your Status is determined by your **current Career level**. Each Career level lists its Status (e.g. "Brass 3", "Silver 1"). When you change Career, note the new Status on your character sheet and take a moment to explain what changed and how it feels for your character.

---

### Changing Status

Status changes when:
1. You **change Career**.
2. You gain a **Talent** that modifies Status.
3. The **GM** imposes a change due to in-game events.

---

### Effects of Status

Status modifies social interactions. The GM may expand or restrict these influences as needed.

| Skill | Effect |
|---|---|
| **[Charm](/codex/skills/charm)** | Higher Tier: **+10** to Charm Tests influencing lower-Tier characters. Lower Tier: **–10** to Charm Tests influencing higher-Tier characters. GM may apply modifiers within the same Tier for different Standings. |
| **[Entertain](/codex/skills/entertain)** | Status does not normally modify Entertain Tests, but the GM should consider whether a performance is suited to its audience and apply appropriate modifiers. |
| **[Intimidate](/codex/skills/intimidate)** | If you have a higher Status than your target: **+10**. |
| **[Gossip](/codex/skills/gossip)** | Tests between individuals of **different Tiers** suffer **–10**. |
| **[Leadership](/codex/skills/leadership)** | One Tier higher than target: **+10**. Two Tiers higher: **+20**. |

> **Optional Rule — Begging & Status:** Begging is most effective when aimed at those just above you. Most Beggars (Brass Tier) gain **+10** when begging from Silver Tier individuals, rather than suffering the normal –10 penalty.

---

### Beyond the Social Norm

Not all NPCs follow Status conventions. When needed, roll **1d10**:

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | **Defies Status.** Ignores all Status effects entirely. |
| 3–8 | **Standard Reactions.** Status rules apply as normal. |
| 9–10 | **Extreme Views.** Modify all Status-influenced Tests by a further **±10** as appropriate. |

---

### Maintaining Status

To benefit from Status, a character must **act in accordance with their social role**. A character going incognito is treated as having a Status of **Brass 3** regardless of their true standing.

> **Example:** Hans the noble rides into a village in fine fashion — full Gold 2 Status applies. His brother Heinrich, dressed in rags and acting anonymous, is treated as Brass 3.

**Keeping Up Appearances:** Characters must spend a minimum amount of money each week to sustain their Status (see [The Cost of Living](/codex/consumers-guide/cost-of-living)). Failing to do so causes a loss of **1 Standing per week**. Resuming appropriate spending recovers 1 Standing per week. If Standing reaches **0**, the Tier drops by one and Standing resets to **5**. At Brass Tier with 0 Standing, Status is fixed at **Brass 0**.

> **Example:** Pietr is a Noble at Gold 3. After two weeks slumming it in Altdorf's East End, his Standing has dropped to Gold 1. One more week of poverty and he becomes Silver 5 — likely prompting his father's intervention.
