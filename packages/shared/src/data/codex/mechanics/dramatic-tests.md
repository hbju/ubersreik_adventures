## Dramatic Tests

A **Dramatic Test** is used when knowing *how well* a character succeeds or fails matters — particularly in combat, magic, or when the story's direction depends on the outcome.

Dramatic Tests resolve the same way as Simple Tests but also produce **Success Levels (SL)**.

### Success Levels (SL)

Subtract the **tens digit of the roll** from the **tens digit of the tested Skill** (including any modifiers). The result is the **SL**.

- A **positive SL** indicates success; higher is better.
- A **negative SL** indicates failure; lower is worse.

> **Example:** Eichengard tests **Ride (Horse)** at 41. He rolls 29. The tens digit of his skill is **4**; the tens digit of the roll is **2**. Result: **+2 SL** — a solid success as he clears the wall.
>
> Moments later, he tests **Perception** at 39 and rolls 82. Tens digit of skill is **3**, tens digit of roll is **8**. Result: **–5 SL** — an Impressive Failure. A low-hanging branch slams into him as he was busy gloating.

### Outcomes Table

| SL | Result | Success? |
|---|---|---|
| +6 or more | Astounding Success | Yes, perfectly! |
| +4 to +5 | Impressive Success | Yes, and… |
| +2 to +3 | Success | Yes |
| +0 to +1 | Marginal Success | Yes, but… |
| –0 to –1 | Marginal Failure | No, but… |
| –2 to –3 | Failure | No |
| –4 to –5 | Impressive Failure | No, and… |
| –6 or less | Astounding Failure | No, not in any way! |

- If you **auto-succeed** (01–05), score **+1 SL** or the rolled SL, whichever is higher.
- If you **auto-fail** (96–00), score **–1 SL** or the rolled SL, whichever is lower.

> **Optional Rule — Fast SL:** When you pass a test, use the **tens die result** directly as your SL. A passing roll of 36 = **+3 SL**. Failed tests still calculate SL normally (skill tens minus roll tens, as a negative).

> **Optional Rule — Criticals & Fumbles (All Tests):** Any successful Test that also rolls a double is a **Critical** (Astounding Success). Any failed Test rolling a double is a **Fumble** (Astounding Failure).
