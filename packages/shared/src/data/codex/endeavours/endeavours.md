## Between Adventures

> 'Of course there is something wrong with us. This world has more than enough sorrow and pain to go about. So, what do we do? We seek out things that even the courageous fear and confront them in the dark. Truly, we are the favoured pawns of the Gods. So: what do I do with my time when I'm not on some foolish quest? I drink.'
> — Lieselotte Aderhold, Adventurer

When an adventure concludes, characters may have weeks of downtime before the next one begins. This chapter covers how to resolve that time efficiently.

The sequence is:

1. Roll on the **Events Table**.
2. Spend money earned during the last adventure.
3. Select and resolve **Endeavours**.
4. Carry over any remaining saved funds (via **[Banking](/codex/endeavours/banking)**) or starting income (via **[Income](/codex/endeavours/income)**).

> **Optional Rule:** Every rule in this chapter is optional. Some groups prefer to roll through it quickly; others use the downtime as a springboard for roleplay and new adventures. There is no wrong approach.

---

## Events

Before selecting Endeavours, roll **1d100** on the Events Table. Some results affect only the rolling character; others affect the entire party.

## Money to Burn

After rolling on the Events Table, spend any money earned during the last adventure using normal rules. Once all Endeavours are resolved, **all remaining money is lost** — spent on living expenses, debts, taxes, bribes, donations, or whatever best fits your character's story.

To preserve funds for the future, use the **[Banking](/codex/endeavours/banking)** Endeavour. To ensure starting funds for the next adventure, use the **[Income](/codex/endeavours/income)** Endeavour.

---

## Endeavours

Between adventures, each character may undertake **a maximum of one Endeavour per week** of downtime, up to a **maximum of three Endeavours total**, regardless of how long the gap between adventures actually is. The rest of the time is spent living life.

> **Flying Solo:** Endeavours are designed for individual characters. Where it makes narrative sense for multiple characters to collaborate, roleplay it — and watch for Endeavours that might spark new adventures entirely.

> **Year In, Year Out:** For especially long gaps between adventures, the GM may expand the narrative scope of each Endeavour — sourcing materials, navigating setbacks, waiting in queues — to fill the time more richly.
